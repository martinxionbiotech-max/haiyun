## The comparison

| Criterion | Roller mill | Stone mill |
|---|---|---|
| Roller/stone speed | 550 rpm | 28–30 rpm |
| Output per kW | High | 15–25% of roller |
| kWh per tonne | 65–75 | 105–135 |
| Flour temperature | 45–60 °C | +5–15 °C above ambient |
| Carotenoid retention | Partial loss | Near complete |
| Vitamin E retention | Partial loss | Near complete |
| Extraction (white flour) | 72–80% | 45–60% (uneconomic) |
| Extraction (whole wheat) | 85–88% | 85–92% |
| Particle size | Narrow, fine | Wide, coarser |
| Capital per t/d capacity | Baseline | 4–6× |
| Labour per tonne | Low | High |
| Shelf life (whole wheat) | 3–6 months | 3–6 months |
| Consistency | High | Medium |

## Where roller wins decisively

**White patent flour.** To make white flour you must separate bran from endosperm *before* grinding fine. Roller mills do this across multiple passages with intermediate sifting. Stone mills cannot — they grind everything together.

**Scale.** A 500 t/d roller plant exists. A 500 t/d stone plant would need 800 stone mills.

**Consistency.** Roller milling produces a predictable particle size distribution. Stone milling varies with stone condition, which is a variable most mills manage poorly.

**Energy per tonne.** 70 versus 120 kWh/t is a decisive cost difference at commodity prices.

## Where stone wins

**Nutrient retention.** Cold grinding preserves carotenoids, vitamin E, B vitamins and gluten integrity. This is measurable and it is the entire commercial premise.

**Whole wheat character.** Stone-milled whole wheat flour has larger bran flakes and a coarser, more flavoursome grind. In artisan baking this produces better volume and a more open crumb than finely ground roller wholemeal.

**Market positioning.** "Stone ground" is a recognised premium claim that consumers pay 1.5–2× for. There is no equivalent claim available to a roller mill.

**Simplicity.** Fewer moving parts, fewer adjustments, easier for a small operator to run.

## The honest verdict

| If you are… | Choose |
|---|---|
| Making white patent flour at any scale | Roller |
| Making Atta / whole wheat for a commodity market | Roller (higher output, lower cost) |
| Making premium whole wheat for health-food channels | Stone or hybrid |
| A bakery wanting a signature flour | Stone (SFM-100) |
| Entering the premium market without abandoning yield | Hybrid SFM-1+2 |
| Building above 20 t/d of white flour | Roller, no question |

Most commercial buyers who want stone character end up with the hybrid. It is the configuration that acknowledges both economics and positioning.
