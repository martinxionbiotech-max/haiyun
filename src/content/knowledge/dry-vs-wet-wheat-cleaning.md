## The comparison

| Criterion | Dry cleaning | Wet cleaning |
|---|---|---|
| Capital multiplier | 1.0 | 1.45–1.70 |
| Water use | ~0 | 0.8–1.2 m³/t |
| Effluent | None | Requires treatment |
| Ash improvement | Baseline | 0.03–0.08 points |
| Pesticide residue | Reduced | Effectively removed |
| Microbial load | Reduced 40–60% | Reduced 90%+ |
| Crease cleaning | Partial | Complete |
| Drying load | None | +3–5% moisture to remove |
| Operator complexity | Low | High |
| Footprint | Baseline | +35% |

## Dry cleaning, done properly

Dry cleaning plus two scourer passes with an intervening brush reaches:

- Ash reduction of 0.06–0.10 points versus single-pass
- Microbial load reduction of 40–60%
- Stone removal above 95%

That combination is sufficient for flour sold as a commodity into a domestic bakery market. Most mills in most countries operate this way, and it is the correct economic choice for them.

## Wet cleaning, done properly

A washer-destoner-dryer:

1. Floods the wheat in a washing trough
2. Floats off light impurities
3. Sinks stones for separate discharge
4. Scours the crease with jets
5. Centrifuges surface water to 15–16% moisture

The result is wheat that is visibly and measurably cleaner. The crease — a fold that runs the length of every kernel — is the only place where a dry process cannot reach, and wet cleaning is the only process that does.

## The decision framework

**Choose dry when:**
- Your market is domestic commodity flour
- Wheat arrives with low visible soil
- Water is scarce or expensive
- Effluent discharge is not permitted
- Capital is constrained

**Choose wet when:**
- You export to regulated markets
- Your buyers test residue or microbial load
- Your wheat arrives with visible crease soil
- You compete on flour colour and ash at the premium end
- Water and effluent treatment are available

## The hidden cost: effluent

Washing water carries soil, organic matter and pesticide residue. Discharging it untreated is illegal almost everywhere and will eventually be detected.

Budget for:

| Item | Cost (30 t/d plant) |
|---|---|
| Settling pit (3-chamber) | USD 8,000–14,000 |
| Recirculation pump set | USD 3,000–5,000 |
| Filtration | USD 6,000–12,000 |
| Annual maintenance | USD 2,000–4,000 |

That is 15–20% of the wet-cleaning capital and 100% of your ability to keep operating legally.

## A middle path

Many plants install dry cleaning plus:

- Two scourer passes instead of one
- A wheat brush machine (TNSW series) for kernel-by-kernel surface cleaning
- An additional aspirator before the first break

This combination reaches roughly 70% of wet cleaning's ash benefit at 30% of the capital, with no water and no effluent. For a mill that needs better flour but cannot justify wet cleaning, it is the sensible compromise.
