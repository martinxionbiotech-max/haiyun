## The five classes that matter commercially

| Class | Protein | Hardness | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft white / soft red | 8.0–10.0% | Soft | Biscuit, cake, pastry |
| Hard red winter | 11.0–12.5% | Hard | Pan bread, general bakery |
| Hard red spring | 12.5–14.5% | Very hard | Hearth bread, strong flour, blending stock |
| Durum | 12.5–14.5% | Very hard, vitreous | Semolina, pasta |
| Local feed / general | 9.0–11.5% | Mixed | Steamed bread, flatbread, noodles |

Local names vary by country — atta wheat, maida wheat, chakki wheat — but the underlying parameters are the same three: protein content, protein quality and hardness.

## The five contract parameters

### 1. Moisture

| Level | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Below 11.5% | Wheat is brittle; more broken kernels, lower extraction |
| 12.0–13.0% | Ideal for milling and storage |
| 13.0–14.0% | Acceptable short term; tempering water reduced accordingly |
| Above 14.0% | Mould risk; price must be adjusted on dry matter |

Always price on **dry matter**, not wet weight. A 14% moisture load priced at the 12.5% rate gives the seller 1.7% extra wheat for free.

### 2. Protein

Measured by Kjeldahl (N × 5.7 for wheat) or NIR, expressed at 14% moisture basis.

Write the contract as target ±0.3% with pro-rata adjustment, not pass/fail. A cliff clause means a 0.1% shortfall rejects a whole truck, which costs everyone more than a price adjustment.

| Clause form | Effect |
|---|---|
| "12.0% min, reject below" | Supplier ships 12.05%; you never see 12.5% |
| "12.0% ±0.3%, pro-rata" | Supplier targets 12.0%; you get consistency |
| "12.0–12.6% band, price by formula" | Best for blending operations |

### 3. Test weight

76 kg/hl minimum for bread wheat, 74 for general purpose. Below 72 the wheat is likely shrivelled or heat-damaged and will underperform in the mill.

### 4. Falling number

250 s minimum. This is the one parameter with no recovery path.

| Falling number | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Above 300 s | Sound, dormant | Normal |
| 250–300 s | Acceptable | Monitor |
| 200–250 s | Mild sprout damage | Blend up to 20% max |
| Below 200 s | Significant sprout damage | Reject |

Alpha-amylase from sprouting degrades starch into dextrins. Bread made from it has a sticky crumb that collapses. Milling cannot remove the enzyme because it is distributed throughout the endosperm.

### 5. Impurity

| Fraction | Limit |
|---|---|
| Total impurities | 1.5% max |
| Foreign matter (stones, metal, glass) | 0.5% max |
| Other grain | 1.0% max |
| Broken kernels | 3.0% max |
| Ergots and sclerotia | 0.05% max |

Impurity above specification is a cleaning cost, not a milling cost. Every extra 1% of impurity is roughly 1% of intake capacity spent moving material you did not buy.

## What is usually omitted and should not be

| Parameter | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Damaged kernels | Heat-damaged wheat produces dark specks in flour |
| Vitreousness | Affects roll settings and semolina yield |
| Pesticide residue | Regulatory compliance for export markets |
| Heavy metals | Cadmium limits in EU and Japan |
| Mycotoxins (DON) | 1,250 ppb EU limit for unprocessed wheat |

For export flour, DON and cadmium are the two that stop shipments.

## Sampling: where contracts are won and lost

The contract means nothing without a sampling protocol.

| Practice | Standard |
|---|---|
| Sampling method | Mechanical probe, 5–8 cores per truck |
| Sample division | Quartering or riffling, never hand-scoop |
| Reference sample | Retained, sealed, 500 g per lot, 90 days |
| Dispute resolution | Independent laboratory, cost to the losing party |

Hand-scooping from the top of a truck samples the driest, cleanest wheat in the load. It is the single most common way buyers get cheated and the easiest thing to fix.
