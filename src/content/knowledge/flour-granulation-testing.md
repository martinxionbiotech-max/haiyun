Two flours with identical protein and ash can bake completely differently, and granulation is usually why. It is set by roll gap, sifter aperture and detacher condition — three variables that drift quietly between services. It is also the first thing to check when a customer says the flour changed but the analysis sheet looks the same.

## Why it matters

Ash and protein tell you what is in the flour. Granulation tells you how it will behave.

Two flours at 0.55% ash and 12% protein can produce completely different bread if one has 30% of particles under 60 µm and the other has 15%. The fine fraction hydrates in seconds and is available to yeast immediately; the coarse fraction hydrates over minutes and colours the crust.

This is why "the flour changed" complaints are so often traceable to granulation drift rather than composition.

## Testing

**Equipment**: sieve shaker + stack of 100, 150, 200, 250 mesh (150, 100, 75, 63 µm) sieves.

**Method**:

1. Weigh 100 g of flour
2. Stack sieves coarse to fine with a pan at the bottom
3. Shake 10 minutes
4. Weigh each fraction
5. Report cumulative % passing each screen

**Typical report**:

| Screen | Passing |
|---|---|
| 100 mesh (150 µm) | 99.5% |
| 150 mesh (100 µm) | 92% |
| 200 mesh (75 µm) | 78% |
| 250 mesh (63 µm) | 61% |

## Targets by application

| Application | Passing 200 mesh | Distribution |
|---|---|---|
| Bread | 85–95% | Broad acceptable |
| Noodle | 85–95% | Narrow, uniform |
| Biscuit | 80–90% | Moderate |
| Cake | 95%+ | Very fine |
| Atta | 70–85% | Coarse, deliberate |
| Semolina | <10% (different product) | 250–600 µm |

## What controls it

| Control | Effect |
|---|---|
| Reduction roll gap | Smaller gap = finer flour |
| Reduction roll differential | Higher differential = more fines and more bran dust |
| Sifter screen aperture on flour passages | Directly sets cut point |
| Impact detacher | Breaks flakes, increasing passing fraction 5–12% |
| Roll condition | Dull rolls produce coarser, more variable flour |

## The detacher effect

Smooth reduction rolls produce flat flakes. Those flakes behave as large particles in a sieve test but are actually thin and fragile.

An [impact detacher](/equipment/impact-detacher/) breaks them apart, which:

- Increases the passing fraction by 5–12%
- Improves sifting efficiency
- Produces a more uniform distribution

Without a detacher, your granulation test may report a coarse flour that actually bakes fine — or a fine flour that bakes coarse. The measurement is misleading because flake dimension depends on orientation.

## Drift diagnosis

| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Granulation coarser over weeks | Roll wear or fluting |
| Granulation suddenly coarser | Screen frame torn |
| Granulation suddenly finer | Roll gap closed or screen changed |
| Increased variability | Detacher pins worn, or feed rate unstable |
| Coarse fraction with high ash | Bran flakes — check break rolls and sifter |

## Practical discipline

Test granulation:

- On every product change
- When a customer complains about behaviour
- Monthly as a trend check
- After any roll change or screen replacement

Do not test every shift. It consumes 20 minutes and rarely changes. Test when something is different.
