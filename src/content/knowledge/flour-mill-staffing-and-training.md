Labour is only 4–8% of operating cost, but the head miller decides 20–30% of your extraction rate. That asymmetry explains why the most dangerous staffing structure in a flour mill is one person holding undocumented knowledge of how the plant actually runs — not one person holding a key role.

## Headcount by capacity

| Capacity | Production | Maintenance | Lab/QC | Admin/sales | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 t/d | 2–3 | 1 (shared) | 0.5 | 1–2 | 4–6 |
| 60 t/d | 5–7 | 1–2 | 1 | 2–3 | 9–13 |
| 100 t/d | 8–10 | 2–3 | 2 | 3–4 | 15–19 |
| 200 t/d | 15–18 | 3–4 | 3 | 4–6 | 25–31 |
| 500 t/d | 30–40 | 6–9 | 5–7 | 8–12 | 49–68 |

The rule of thumb: **one employee per 10–15 t/d** across the whole business, with larger plants at the efficient end.

## The roles that matter

### Head miller

The most consequential person in the plant, and the one most often hired last.

| Responsibility | Consequence of getting it wrong |
|---|---|
| Roll gap setting | 2–5 points of extraction |
| Flow sheet balance | Ash specification, throughput |
| Sifter screen selection | Granulation, sifting efficiency |
| Purifier setting | Semolina purity, ash |
| Adjustment to wheat change | Everything downstream |
| Quality troubleshooting | Customer rejections |

What a head miller actually does cannot be written in a manual: judging stock by squeezing it, listening to a sifter, knowing that this wheat needs 0.03 mm less gap than the last load. It is built over 5–10 years.

**Two head millers is not a luxury at 200 t/d. It is risk management.** Mills with one head miller and no deputy lose 3–9 months of performance when that person leaves.

### Shift miller / operator

Runs the plant on shift. Monitors, adjusts within limits, responds to alarms, records.

| Skill | Training time |
|---|---|
| Start-up and shutdown sequence | 2–4 weeks |
| Normal operating parameters | 1–2 months |
| Alarm response | 3–6 months |
| Minor adjustment within limits | 6–12 months |
| Independent judgement | 2–3 years |

### Maintenance technician

| Discipline | Per 100 t/d |
|---|---|
| Mechanical | 1.5 |
| Electrical | 0.7 |
| Instrumentation/automation | 0.3 |

Mechanical dominates because bearings, belts, screens and spouting are the bulk of the work. Instrumentation is increasingly needed as PLCs and sensors proliferate — a mill with 200 sensors and nobody who understands them has 200 liabilities.

### Laboratory technician

| Test | Skill level |
|---|---|
| Moisture, ash | Basic, 2 weeks training |
| Protein (Kjeldahl/NIR) | Intermediate, 1 month |
| Falling number | Basic, 2 weeks |
| Farinograph | Intermediate, 1 month |
| Extensograph | Intermediate, 1 month |
| Alveograph | Advanced, 2 months |
| Baking test | Advanced, 3–6 months |

A mill producing to specification needs at least one person who can run a farinograph and interpret the curve. Without it, the mill is producing to a number it cannot verify.

Labour is 4–8% of operating cost in most markets — low enough that cutting headcount is rarely the right response to a cost problem, and high enough that productivity matters.

## Training structure

### The four levels

| Level | Content | Duration | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Induction | Safety, plant layout, emergency procedures | 1 week | Everyone |
| 2. Role | Specific equipment and procedures | 1–3 months | Everyone |
| 3. Cross-training | Adjacent roles and the wider flow sheet | 6–12 months | Operators |
| 4. Judgement | Flow sheet behaviour, quality diagnosis | Years | Potential head millers |

Most mills do levels 1 and 2 and stop. Levels 3 and 4 are what produce people who can run the plant when something unusual happens.

### What level 4 looks like

A head miller diagnosing a problem thinks like this:

1. Ash is up 0.04% and extraction is down 1.5 points.
2. Check whether wheat changed — if yes, adjust dampening.
3. If wheat unchanged, check break release. Break 1 release down means flutes worn or gap drifted.
4. Check sifter tailings. Rising tailings on 2M means screen blinded.
5. Check [purifier](/equipment/purifier/). Reduced semolina purity raises ash in the patent stream.
6. Only then adjust.

An untrained operator's version: close all the gaps and hope.

### Documentation as training

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Flow sheet with all passages | The plant on one page |
| Operating parameters by passage | Normal ranges |
| Roll gap specification by passage | What to set and why |
| Sifter screen specification by passage | What mesh, why |
| Troubleshooting flowchart | Symptom → cause → action |
| Start-up and shutdown procedure | Written, signed |
| Emergency procedures | Fire, dust, injury, power loss |

A mill whose knowledge lives only in the head miller's head does not have a training problem. It has a continuity problem, and it will surface the day that person is unavailable.

## Shift patterns

| Pattern | Hours | Recovery | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8-hour, 3 shifts | 24 h coverage | Good | Standard |
| 12-hour, 2 shifts | 24 h coverage | Fatigue risk | Fewer handovers, more fatigue |
| 24/5 with weekend shutdown | 120 h/week | Full weekend | Maintenance window |
| 24/7 continuous | 168 h/week | Rotating | Needs more staff, higher cost |

Most 60–200 t/d mills run 24/5 with a weekend maintenance window. It aligns maintenance with low demand and avoids the cost and error rate of night-shift-only operations.

Handover discipline matters more than the pattern. A 10-minute structured handover — what changed, what is abnormal, what is pending — prevents more failures than any amount of training.

## The staffing risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Single point of knowledge (head miller) | Hire a deputy, document everything |
| No instrumentation skills | Train one technician or contract it |
| No lab capability | Shared lab arrangement with a nearby mill or a service lab |
| Operator turnover above 15%/yr | Pay above local average, train visibly, create progression |
| No cross-training | Deliberate rotation, annual |
| Aging workforce | Apprentice programme, 3–5 year horizon |

The most common failure in new mills is not equipment. It is a plant with no one who can set it up, staffed by people who were trained by the installer for two weeks and then left alone.
