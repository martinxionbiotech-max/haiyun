## Why the destoner is the machine people get wrong

Screens are intuitive: wheat passes over holes, small things fall through. Destoners are not. They separate particles of the same size by density, using a fluidised bed created by air passing up through a vibrating deck.

Three parameters are coupled:

| Parameter | Effect |
|---|---|
| Air volume | Fluidises the bed; too little and nothing separates, too much and wheat blows out with the stones |
| Deck amplitude | Agitates the bed; too little and it does not stratify |
| Deck angle | Controls residence time and stone travel direction |

Change one and you must re-tune the other two. This is why destoners are often found running badly — someone adjusted the air "to stop blowing wheat out" and never re-tuned the rest.

Note the bold rows: TQSFB4125 delivers 64% more capacity than TQSF125 on the identical 125 cm width. The difference is air volume and deck geometry.

**When to choose a B variant:** when floor space is the constraint rather than headroom, or when you are retrofitting a higher-capacity line into an existing building.

**When not to:** B variants need 20% more air, which means a larger fan and more power. If space is available, the standard model is more efficient per tonne.

## Sizing rule

**Destoner capacity ≥ 1.3 × cleaning line throughput**

A 30 t/d plant with cleaning at 5 t/h needs 6.5 t/h of destoner capacity → TQSF100.

Do not size on mill throughput. Cleaning runs ahead of milling, and during harvest intake the line may run at 1.5× nominal for hours.

## Air supply

The destoner's fan is usually supplied with the machine, but check:

- **Static pressure** must match the deck resistance at the specified air volume
- **Duct length** beyond 3 m adds pressure drop and reduces effective volume
- **Air quality** — dusty recirculated air blinds the deck and reduces fluidisation

If you share a fan with other equipment, verify the destoner still gets its rated volume. Shared fans are a common cause of poor destoning.

## The 95% field test

Quarterly, do this:

1. Count out exactly 200 clean field stones (or glass beads of similar size)
2. Mix them into 20 kg of clean wheat
3. Run the wheat through the destoner at production rate
4. Collect and count stones in the stone outlet and in the accepted wheat

Target: **190+ stones in the stone outlet, fewer than 10 in the accepted wheat** (95% removal).

Results and actions:

| Removal rate | Action |
|---|---|
| > 97% | Excellent |
| 95–97% | Acceptable |
| 90–95% | Re-tune air volume and deck angle |
| < 90% | Inspect screen cloth for wear or holes; replace if needed |

Wheat loss in the stone outlet should be under 10 kernels per 100 stones. More than that means the air volume is too high and you are blowing good wheat out with the stones.

## Maintenance

| Item | Interval |
|---|---|
| Deck screen inspection | Weekly |
| Screen replacement | When holes or wear exceed 10% of area |
| Air volume check | Monthly |
| Fan bearing grease | 500 hours |
| Suspension springs | Annual inspection |

The deck screen is the wearing part. A hole in the deck lets wheat fall into the stone channel, which shows up as high wheat loss in the stone outlet — a symptom often misdiagnosed as excessive air.
