Dress stones every 300–500 operating hours, depending on wheat and load. Undressed stones smear instead of cut, flour temperature climbs and colour darkens. Because the decline is gradual and invisible, the only reliable trigger is a logged schedule plus hourly stone temperature — not the operator's judgement.

## Why dressing matters

A millstone is a cutting tool, not a grinding surface. Its face has furrows — grooves cut in a pattern from the centre to the edge — that shear grain as the stone turns.

Over time the furrows round off. The stone then **rubs** instead of cutting. The visible symptoms are subtle and the invisible ones are expensive.

## Interval

| Condition | Interval |
|---|---|
| Soft wheat, light load, <6 h/day | 500 hours |
| Mixed wheat, 8 h/day | 400 hours |
| Hard wheat, continuous | 300 hours |
| Whole wheat, high bran load | 250–300 hours |
| Flour temperature > 45 °C | Immediately |
| Visible flour colour darkening | Immediately |

## What goes wrong

| Stage | Stone condition | Flour |
|---|---|---|
| 0–200 h | Sharp furrows | Pale, cool, correct granulation |
| 200–350 h | Slightly rounded | Slightly warmer, marginally coarser |
| 350–500 h | Rounded | Warmer, darker, coarser |
| 500–700 h | Polished | Hot, noticeably darker, uneven |
| > 700 h | Glazed | Burning smell possible, unacceptable |

The critical point: **there is no mechanical noise, no vibration, no alarm.** The only signals are flour temperature, colour and granulation — all of which drift slowly enough that operators normalise them.

## The temperature rule

Log stone surface temperature hourly.

| Temperature | Status |
|---|---|
| Ambient + 5 to +12 °C | Normal, sharp stone |
| Ambient + 12 to +18 °C | Getting dull, plan dressing |
| Ambient + 18 to +25 °C | Dull, dress now |
| Above 45 °C absolute | Stop and dress immediately |

A cheap infrared thermometer is the most valuable instrument in a [stone mill](/flour-milling-plant/stone-mill-unit/).

## Dressing method

1. **Isolate and lock out** the mill. Stones must not turn.
2. **Remove the casing** and expose the stone face.
3. **Inspect the furrow pattern** against the original template.
4. **Re-cut the furrows** with a dressing hammer and chisel, or a powered dressing tool for larger stones.
5. **Level the face** — high spots cause uneven wear and vibration.
6. **Clean out** all stone dust and chips before reassembly.
7. **Run-in** for 30–60 minutes with sacrificial grain, discarding the flour.
8. **Verify** temperature and granulation against baseline.

Time: 2–5 hours depending on stone size and condition.

## Record keeping

Keep a stone log:

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Date and hours since last dressing | Predicts next dressing |
| Wheat type and moisture | Explains wear rate variation |
| Stone temperature at start | Baseline |
| Furrow depth measurements | Objective wear metric |
| Operator | Accountability |

Without a log, dressing happens when someone remembers. With a log, it happens on schedule and flour quality stops drifting.
