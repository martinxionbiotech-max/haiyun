Wheat arrives with six classes of impurity, and each needs a different separation principle — which is why no single machine handles more than one or two of them properly. The class that most often escapes undersized cleaning lines is field stone: same size as wheat, twice the density, invisible to any screen.

## Class 1 — Coarse impurities

**Examples**: straw, rope, cob, string, plastic, wood, stones larger than 8 mm.

**Removed by**: [drum sieve](/equipment/drum-sieve-pre-cleaner/) (SCQY series).

**Typical level**: 0.1–0.8% depending on harvest method. Combine-harvested wheat carries more straw than hand-harvested.

**Why it matters**: beyond the obvious, coarse material blocks screens and wraps shafts. This is a machine-protection stage, not a quality stage.

## Class 2 — Size-graded impurities

**Examples**: sand, small stones, broken grain, weed seeds, chaff fragments.

**Removed by**: vibrating screen (TQLZ, HZXZ) or rotary screen.

**Typical level**: 0.5–2.0%.

**Why it matters**: sand that survives into the mill abrades roll flutes. Weed seeds add off-flavours and, in the case of ergot, toxins.

## Class 3 — Density impurities

**Examples**: field stones, glass, mud balls, coal, brick fragments.

**Removed by**: [gravity destoner](/equipment/gravity-destoner/) (TQSF).

**Typical level**: 0.05–0.5%.

**Why it matters most**: field stone has almost exactly the size of a wheat kernel and roughly twice the density. Screens cannot separate it. Only combined airflow and vibration can. An undersized destoner removes almost none and the failure is invisible from outside.

## Class 4 — Surface impurities

**Examples**: dirt, dust, wheat beard, smut spores, bacteria, mould spores, pesticide residue.

**Removed by**: [scourer](/equipment/horizontal-wheat-scourer/) (FDMW), brush machine (TNSW), aspirator (TXFY).

**Typical level**: variable; measured as ash contribution.

**Why it matters**: this is the class that determines flour ash. A single heavy-beat scourer pass removes 0.03–0.06 points of ash from the whole meal.

## Class 5 — Shape and length impurities

**Examples**: broken kernels, shrivelled grain, ergot bodies, sprouted kernels, insect-damaged kernels.

**Removed by**: indent cylinder (MDJY).

**Typical level**: 2–8%.

**Why it matters**: broken kernels mill differently from whole ones and raise ash. Ergot is a food-safety issue with legal limits in most markets.

## Class 6 — Ferrous impurities

**Examples**: wire, bolts, staples, screen fragments, wear particles from machinery.

**Removed by**: plate magnets (TCXP) and drum magnets (TCXT).

**Typical level**: trace.

**Why it matters**: this is the only impurity class that can be introduced *by* the plant. Every screen and every roll is a potential source of ferrous fragments.

## Typical impurity profile by origin

| Origin | Total impurities | Stones | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Well-cleaned commercial wheat | 0.8–1.5% | 0.05–0.15% | Standard European or North American grade |
| Average farm wheat | 1.5–3.0% | 0.15–0.4% | Requires full cleaning line |
| Rain-damaged / late harvest | 3.0–6.0% | 0.2–0.6% | Expect sprout damage too |
| Imported blending wheat | 1.0–2.0% | 0.1–0.3% | Varies widely by origin |

## What each class contributes to ash

Approximate ash contribution per 1% of impurity in the incoming wheat:

| Class | Ash contribution |
|---|---|
| Coarse organic | 0.00 (removed before it matters) |
| Sand and small stones | 0.00–0.02 (removed by screens and destoner) |
| Surface dirt | 0.05–0.12 (the dominant contributor) |
| Broken kernels | 0.02–0.04 |
| Bran fines | 0.04–0.08 |

Note that surface dirt dominates. Which is why scourers and aspirators — not screens — are the machines that determine flour ash.

## Testing your own wheat

A simple sieve-and-float test tells you most of what you need:

1. Weigh 1 kg of representative sample
2. Sieve through 2 mm and 1 mm screens; weigh each fraction
3. Float the 2–4 mm fraction in salt solution (1.2 specific gravity) — stones sink, wheat floats
4. Hand-pick and weigh coarse material

Ten minutes of work gives you an impurity profile accurate enough to specify a cleaning line. Most buyers skip this and accept whatever the supplier recommends.
