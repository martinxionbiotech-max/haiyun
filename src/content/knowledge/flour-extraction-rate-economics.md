## Definitions

**Extraction rate** = flour produced ÷ wheat milled × 100.

**Yield** is sometimes used interchangeably but strictly means saleable product (including bran) per unit input, which is always higher.

Stick to extraction for flour.

## Typical ranges

| Product | Extraction | Ash |
|---|---|---|
| Patent only (high grade) | 65–72% | 0.45–0.55% |
| Patent + standard | 75–78% | 0.55–0.70% |
| Standard / clear | 78–82% | 0.75–0.95% |
| Atta / whole wheat | 85–92% | 1.20–1.80% |
| Maida (South Asian) | 72–76% | 0.50–0.60% |

## Where extraction comes from

| Source | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Break passages (each beyond 2) | +1.0–2.0 points |
| Purifier | +5–8 points (at lower ash) |
| Bran finisher | +1.5–3.0 points |
| Tailing passage | +0.5–1.5 points |
| Impact detacher | +0.5–1.0 points (via sifting efficiency) |
| Roll condition | +2–3 points if re-fluted on schedule |
| Operator setting | +0.5–2 points |

Four of these are design decisions made before the plant is built. Two are maintenance. Only one is operator skill.

**That is the key insight: extraction is designed, not operated.**

## The 60 t/d arithmetic

| Extraction | Flour (t/yr) | Revenue at USD 400/t |
|---|---|---|
| 72% | 14,256 | 5,702,400 |
| 74% | 14,652 | 5,860,800 |
| 76% | 15,048 | 6,019,200 |
| 78% | 15,444 | 6,177,600 |
| 80% | 15,840 | 6,336,000 |

Each point ≈ 198 t of flour ≈ USD 79,200 at 60 t/d over 330 days.

Against that:

| Cost item | Annual (60 t/d) |
|---|---|
| Total power bill | ~USD 85,300 |
| Total maintenance | ~USD 12,000 |
| Total labour | ~USD 95,000 |

**One point of extraction is worth roughly the entire power bill.**

## The ash ceiling

Extraction is not free. Beyond a point, additional flour is bran-contaminated and the price you get for it falls faster than the volume rises.

| Extraction | Ash | Price index |
|---|---|---|
| 74% | 0.55% | 100 |
| 77% | 0.62% | 97 |
| 80% | 0.75% | 90 |
| 83% | 0.92% | 80 |

At 83% extraction you produce 4% more flour than at 80% and sell it for 11% less. The revenue line goes down.

The optimum is different for every market. In a market that pays a premium for low ash, 74–76% is optimal. In a market that buys standard flour on price, 78–80% is optimal.

## Three practical improvements

**1. Add a bran finisher.** Recovering 1.5–3% of mill intake as saleable flour generally pays for the machine in under a year. It is the single best retrofit available.

**2. Re-flute on schedule.** Mills that wait for symptoms typically run 2–3 points below design extraction for months before anyone notices.

**3. Check the purifier air balance.** A purifier running out of balance silently loses 2–4 points of extraction. Re-balance costs a few hours of technician time.
