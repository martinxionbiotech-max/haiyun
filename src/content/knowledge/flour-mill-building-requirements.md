Building cost is 20–30% of project capital and almost never appears in an equipment quotation. Getting it wrong is expensive in both directions: too small and the plant will not fit, too tall and you paid for air.

## Three building types

### Shed / single storey — up to 30 t/d

| Capacity | Dimensions | Clear height |
|---|---|---|
| 20 t/d | 12 × 5 m | 4 m |
| 25 t/d | 15 × 5 m | 4 m |
| 30 t/d | 18 × 6 m | 6.5 m |

Floor-mounted twin-bin [plansifters](/equipment/high-square-plansifter/) sit at ground level; product is lifted by bucket elevator or pneumatic line and falls by gravity through short runs.

Advantages: cheapest civil cost, easy maintenance access, simple ventilation, can be built from standard industrial frames.

Limits: multiple re-lifts of product, more pneumatic power, constrained flow sheet complexity.

### Two-floor — 40–60 t/d

| Capacity | Dimensions | Height |
|---|---|---|
| 40 t/d | 18 × 8 m | 8 m |
| 60 t/d | 24 × 8 m | 8 m |

High square plansifters on the upper floor, roll stands below, cleaning on the ground floor with wheat lifted once to the top. Gravity does most of the work.

### Storey-typed tower — 60–500 t/d

| Capacity | Workshop dimensions | Storeys |
|---|---|---|
| 60 t/d | 30 × 7 × 12.5 m | 3–4 |
| 100 t/d | 35 × 7.5 × 18.5 m | 4–5 |
| 200 t/d | 50 × 7.5 × 22.5 m | 5–6 |
| 300 t/d | 60 × 11 × 27.5 m | 6–7 |
| 500 t/d | 55 × 17 × 27.5 m | 6–7 |

Product enters at the top and falls through the entire flow sheet under gravity. This is the most energy-efficient arrangement and the most expensive to build.

## Structural requirements

**Floor loading.** Mill floors carry roll stands (1–3 t each) and plansifters (2.4–4.7 t). Design for 800–1,200 kg/m² uniformly plus point loads and a 1.5× dynamic factor for vibration.

**Vibration isolation.** Plansifters gyrate at 245 rpm. Mount them on isolation pads or a separate structural bay. Transmitting gyratory force into a lightweight frame produces cracking within a season.

**Clear spans.** Roll stands need 2.5 m clear in front for roll removal. Plansifter compartments need 1.2 m clear for sieve frame extraction.

**Headroom for maintenance.** Above every plansifter, allow 1.5 m for hoisting a sieve stack. Above every roll stand, 1 m for lifting rolls.

## Dust and explosion safety

Flour dust is combustible. Kst values typically fall in the 50–100 bar·m/s range, which places flour in the "strong" explosion class.

Requirements in most jurisdictions:

- **Dust zoning**: classified zones around milling, sifting and packing equipment
- **Explosion venting**: relief panels sized to the enclosure volume, vented to a safe direction
- **Electrical classification**: appropriate IP and Ex ratings in classified zones
- **Bonding and earthing**: all conductive equipment bonded, resistance under 10 ohms
- **Dust collection**: extraction at every transfer point, not just at the packer
- **Housekeeping regime**: no accumulation above 1 mm on surfaces

Retrofitting explosion venting to a building not designed for it is often impossible. Design it in.

## Climate considerations

**Hot climates**: ventilation is critical. Roll stands shed significant heat; without airflow, mill floor temperatures exceed 40 °C and operator error rises. Design for 10–15 air changes per hour.

**Cold climates**: freeze protection on water lines to the dampener, and condensation control on pneumatic systems. Wheat stored below 5 °C mills differently.

**Humid climates**: sealed pneumatic conveying prevents moisture pick-up. Flour stored in unsealed buildings gains 0.3–0.8% moisture, which is both a legal and a margin problem.

**Coastal**: stainless or galvanised contact surfaces, and epoxy coating on steelwork. Salt air destroys a mill frame in five years.
