Layout is where a plant's future operating cost is decided. Two plants with identical equipment can differ by 15% in labour and 8% in power purely because of geometry.

## The one-directional principle

Material should never travel backwards. Every reversal means a conveyor, a lift, or a manual move — and each of those has a cost.

A correct layout:

```
Wheat intake (front of building)
    ↓
Weighing
    ↓
Cleaning section (separate room, dust-controlled)
    ↓
Tempering bins (typically elevated)
    ↓
LIFT — one lift to the highest point
    ↓
Milling floor (break → reduction, sequential)
    ↓
Sifting (below or beside milling)
    ↓
Flour collection
    ↓
Packing (front of building, truck access)
    ↓
Bran out (rear, separate access)
```

The single lift is critical. Every plant has one natural high point; everything downstream of it should use gravity.

## Room separation

Three separations that matter:

**Cleaning from milling.** Cleaning generates dust, stones, chaff and water. Milling needs a clean, dry, temperature-stable environment. A wall between them costs almost nothing and prevents a category of contamination problem.

**Milling from packing.** Packing generates bags, tape, pallets and people. Keep them out of the mill floor.

**Wet from dry.** If you have a washer-destoner, it produces humidity and effluent. Isolate it with drainage and a separate extract fan.

## Access dimensions

| Requirement | Dimension |
|---|---|
| In front of roll stands (roll removal) | 2.5 m |
| In front of sifters (frame extraction) | 1.2 m |
| Main walkway | 1.2 m |
| Secondary walkway | 0.9 m |
| Above sifters (hoisting) | 1.5 m |
| Around packer (bag handling) | 2.0 m |
| Truck turning circle | 18 m radius |

These are not luxuries. A roll stand with 1 m clearance takes four hours to change a roll instead of forty minutes, and that maintenance gets deferred indefinitely.

## Service routing

Plan services before machines, because retrofitting pipes through a full mill floor is a nightmare.

- **Compressed air**: ring main around the perimeter, drops at every roll stand. 0.6 MPa, sized for 1.3× peak.
- **Electrical**: cable trays overhead in walkways, never over product streams (leaks drip onto flour).
- **Aspiration ducting**: short, direct runs. Every elbow costs pressure.
- **Water**: to the dampener and cleaning section only. Nowhere else.
- **Fire water**: hydrants at both ends of the building.

## Future expansion

Leave one free bay. Always.

A plant designed at 30 t/d that might reach [60 t/d](/flour-milling-plant/40-60tpd-flour-plant/) should have:

- Wheat intake and weighing sized for 60 t/d
- Packing area sized for 60 t/d
- Electrical room sized for 2× present load
- One empty structural bay for a second sifter
- Truck access for both intake and dispatch at the higher volume

The cost of this forethought is perhaps 6–8% of building cost. The cost of retrofitting it later is 40% of the same building, plus downtime.

## Common layout mistakes

**1. Packing inside the mill floor.** Dust, bags and people in a production environment. Separate it.

**2. Single door for intake and dispatch.** Trucks queue, and wheat dust contaminates finished goods.

**3. No weighbridge.** You cannot control wheat cost without weighing intake. Retrofit is expensive and never quite works.

**4. Sifters against an external wall.** Condensation on the wall in cold weather runs onto the sifter and into flour.

**5. Electrical panels in the cleaning room.** Dust ingress destroys contactors. Put panels in a pressurised or sealed room.

**6. No spares store.** Parts accumulate on the floor, get damaged, and are lost. A 40 m² store pays for itself repeatedly.
