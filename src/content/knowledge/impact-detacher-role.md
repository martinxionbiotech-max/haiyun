A 3 kW detacher can add 8–15% of effective sifting capacity, because smooth [reduction rolls](/knowledge/roller-mill-differential-ratio/) produce flat flakes that behave like large particles and blind the screens. It is the cheapest capacity available on an existing mill, and it is routinely omitted from quotations to make them look cheaper.

## The flake problem

Reduction rolls run smooth and close. Instead of cutting, they compress. The output contains flat, coherent flakes of compacted flour — some as large as 1–2 mm across but only tens of microns thick.

A [sifter](/equipment/double-bin-plansifter/) sees a flake as a large particle. It is rejected and sent back for further reduction, even though the material inside is already finished flour.

The costs:

| Effect | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Finished flour rejected by sifter | Recirculated through a roll unnecessarily |
| Sifter capacity consumed | Reduced effective sifting area |
| Extra grinding | More energy, more heat, more fine bran |
| Lower extraction | Flour leaves in the bran stream |

## What a detacher does

A rotor carrying pins turns at 2,830–2,910 rpm inside a housing also fitted with pins. Stock entering the centre is thrown outward and impacts the pin array, breaking flakes apart.

The energy per particle is small — enough to separate flakes, not enough to grind endosperm further. Bran flakes, which are more flexible, largely survive intact, which is desirable.

The FSJQ51 intensive model has a denser pin array and larger rotor, producing stronger impact. It is used where stock is heavily flaked or where impact disinfestation is required as a secondary function.

## Placement

**Rule: one detacher per group of reduction mills, before the sifter.**

| Configuration | Detachers needed |
|---|---|
| 20 t/d (3B2M) | 1 |
| 30 t/d (3B3M) | 1–2 |
| 60 t/d (4B5M1S1T) | 2–3 |
| 100 t/d | 3–4 |
| 200 t/d+ | 4–6 |

Placement after break passages is unnecessary — break rolls produce granular material, not flakes.

## Maintenance that decides the outcome

The detacher is cheap and low-maintenance, which is exactly why it gets neglected.

| Item | Interval | Consequence of neglect |
|---|---|---|
| Pin inspection | 500 hours | Worn pins stop breaking flakes |
| Pin rotation (180°) | ~1,000 hours | Uneven wear, short life |
| Pin replacement | ~2,000 hours | Total loss of function |
| Rotor balance check | After any pin change | Bearing destruction within weeks |
| Bearing grease | 500 hours | Seizure |

**Pin wear is the silent failure.** Rounded pins still spin, still consume 3–5 kW, and produce almost no detaching effect. The operator sees nothing. The only signal is a sifter that gradually needs more area for the same throughput.

## Verifying it works

Take a sample of detacher outlet and sieve it against a sample of detacher inlet:

| Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Passing fraction rises 8–15% | Working correctly |
| Passing fraction rises < 5% | Pins worn — replace |
| No change | Rotor not turning, or feed bypassing |

Run this check quarterly. It takes fifteen minutes and is the only reliable test.
