Aeration at 0.15–0.3 m³ per minute per tonne is the highest-return investment in grain storage. Wheat above 20 °C respires and loses dry matter — at 30 °C a stored lot can shed half a percent of its mass per month. Four large silos beat twelve small ones on every metric except flexibility.

## Why storage exists at all

A flour mill does not need wheat storage because wheat is difficult to store. It needs storage because wheat price is seasonal.

| Month (typical) | Relative price |
|---|---|
| Harvest, month 0 | 100 |
| Harvest + 3 | 104–108 |
| Harvest + 6 | 108–114 |
| Harvest + 9 | 110–118 |

Buying at harvest and milling through the year is worth 8–18% on the wheat bill. For a [60 t/d](/flour-milling-plant/40-60tpd-flour-plant/) mill consuming 24,000 t/year at USD 300/t, that is USD 570,000–1,300,000 per year.

Storage capacity is not a cost. It is the mechanism that captures that spread.

## Capacity sizing

| Plant | Wheat/day | 30 days | 45 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 t/d | 26 t | 780 t | 1,170 t |
| 60 t/d | 78 t | 2,340 t | 3,510 t |
| 100 t/d | 130 t | 3,900 t | 5,850 t |
| 200 t/d | 260 t | 7,800 t | 11,700 t |
| 500 t/d | 650 t | 19,500 t | 29,250 t |

Below 20 days you cannot buy at harvest. Above 60 days the capital cost outweighs the price gain unless you are buying a specific quality that is only available seasonally.

## Silo types

| Type | Capacity | Cost/t | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Galvanised steel, flat bottom | 100–5,000 t | USD 120–220 | Standard choice |
| Galvanised steel, hopper bottom | 20–500 t | USD 250–450 | Small mills, easy discharge |
| Concrete, slip-formed | 1,000–20,000 t | USD 180–300 | Very large, long life |
| Flat store (warehouse) | Any | USD 60–120 | Cheap, labour-intensive |
| Bag storage | Under 200 t | Lowest | Only for micro mills |

### The diameter effect

Cost per tonne falls steeply with size because wall area per unit volume falls:

| Diameter | Height | Capacity | Relative cost/t |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 m | 6 m | ~75 t | 100 |
| 6 m | 10 m | ~280 t | 68 |
| 8 m | 14 m | ~700 t | 52 |
| 10 m | 18 m | ~1,400 t | 44 |
| 12 m | 20 m | ~2,260 t | 40 |

Four 8 m bins cost roughly half, per tonne, of twelve 4 m bins. The only reason to use small bins is segregation — you need separate lots for blending.

**Practical answer: use the largest bins your blending strategy allows, plus two small bins for trial lots.**

## Aeration: the non-negotiable

Wheat is a living organism. Above 20 °C it respires, consuming dry matter and generating more heat.

| Temperature | Dry matter loss per month |
|---|---|
| 15 °C | Under 0.05% |
| 20 °C | 0.1–0.2% |
| 25 °C | 0.3–0.4% |
| 30 °C | 0.5–0.8% |
| 35 °C+ | 1%+ and rising fast |

A 2,000 t silo at 30 °C loses 10–16 t per month. At USD 300/t that is USD 3,000–4,800 — every month, silently.

### Aeration rates

| Purpose | Rate | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance cooling | 0.15–0.3 m³/min/t | 20–40 hours per cooling cycle |
| Summer holding | 0.3–0.5 m³/min/t | As needed to hold below 20 °C |
| Hot spot correction | 0.5–1.0 m³/min/t | Continuous until resolved |

For a 1,000 t silo at 0.2 m³/min/t, you need a fan delivering 200 m³/min. That is a 7.5–15 kW fan.

### When to aerate

Aerate when ambient air is **at least 5 °C cooler** than the grain. Aerating with warm humid air makes the problem worse — it adds moisture and heat simultaneously.

| Season | Practice |
|---|---|
| Autumn | Cool grain to below 15 °C, then seal |
| Winter | Cool to below 10 °C where climate allows |
| Spring | Seal; do not aerate as ambient warms |
| Summer | Aerate only at night, only if 5 °C cooler |

## Temperature monitoring

Cables with digital sensors at 1.5–2 m vertical intervals, three or more per silo.

| Reading | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Uniform, below 20 °C | Healthy |
| Uniform, 20–25 °C | Aerate at next cool night |
| Local rise of 3–5 °C | Early hot spot — aerate immediately |
| Local rise above 8 °C | Active heating — aerate and prepare to turn |
| Rise near the wall | Condensation — check for leaks |

A hot spot caught at +3 °C costs a night of fan running. Caught at +15 °C it costs the silo.

## Moisture migration

Temperature gradients drive moisture migration: warm grain at the centre, cool grain at the wall, moisture moves to the cold zone and condenses. This is why top layers crust and wall-adjacent grain spoils.

The fix is uniform temperature, which is what aeration delivers. Silos without aeration develop convection cells within weeks of filling.

## Inert atmosphere and sealing

For long storage or organic markets, sealed silos with controlled atmosphere (CO₂ or N₂) hold quality without chemical treatment. Cost is 15–25% above standard silos, justified where fumigation is restricted or where organic certification forbids residues.

## Turning grain

Moving wheat between silos breaks up temperature stratification and kills insect populations mechanically. One turn per season is standard practice where a transfer conveyor exists.

| Practice | Effect |
|---|---|
| One turn per season | Breaks hot spots, disrupts insects |
| Two turns | Better control, more damage and cost |
| No turning | Relies entirely on aeration and fumigation |

Turning causes 0.1–0.3% breakage per pass. That is real money at scale, so turn deliberately, not routinely.
