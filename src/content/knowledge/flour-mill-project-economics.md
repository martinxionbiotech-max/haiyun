Flour milling is a commodity business with a thin margin and a large capital base. That combination means the project either works at the feasibility stage or it does not work at all — you cannot manage your way out of a plant that was sized for a market that does not exist.

This handbook sets out the financial structure honestly, including the parts that suppliers prefer not to discuss.

## The cost of wheat is the whole game

| Cost element | Share of operating cost |
|---|---|
| Wheat | 65–80% |
| Labour | 5–10% |
| Power | 5–8% |
| Maintenance & spares | 3–5% |
| Packaging | 3–5% |
| Overheads & admin | 2–4% |

Nothing else comes close. Which means the two variables that determine profitability are:

1. **The price you pay for wheat**
2. **The extraction rate — how much flour you get per tonne of wheat**

Extraction is the one you control inside the plant. And it is controllable to a degree most buyers do not appreciate.

### The arithmetic of extraction

Take a [60 t/d](/flour-milling-plant/40-60tpd-flour-plant/) plant at 330 operating days.

| Extraction | Flour produced | Wheat consumed |
|---|---|---|
| 72% | 14,256 t/yr | 19,800 t/yr |
| 76% | 15,048 t/yr | 19,800 t/yr |
| 79% | 15,642 t/yr | 19,800 t/yr |

Same wheat, different flour. At USD 400/t flour:

- 72% → USD 5,702,400 revenue
- 76% → USD 6,019,200 revenue
- 79% → USD 6,256,800 revenue

The 76% to 79% step is worth **USD 237,600 a year** — from the same 60 t/day of wheat.

Now compare that to what it costs to get there. Moving from a 3B4M1S flow sheet (74–76%) to a 4B5M1S1T with a [purifier](/equipment/purifier/) bank (77–79%) costs perhaps USD 90,000 extra in equipment.

Payback: under five months.

This is the single most important economic fact in flour milling, and it is why we push buyers toward more passages than they initially request.

## Capital cost structure

Buyers compare equipment quotations. Projects fail on the other 34–37%.

| Component | Share of total project capital |
|---|---|
| Process equipment | 63–66% |
| Building / civil works | 12–18% |
| Electrical infrastructure | 4–6% |
| Installation & erection | 5–9% |
| Engineering & project management | 2–4% |
| Commissioning & training | 1–2% |
| Contingency | 3–5% |

Mid-points sum to 100%. The ranges are independent — a project at the top of every band would total more than 100%, which is why contingency is the line that absorbs the difference.
| **Working capital (separate)** | **12–18% of the above** |

A buyer who receives a USD 400,000 equipment quotation and budgets USD 450,000 for the project is 25–30% short — the equipment is only two thirds of what has to be paid for. This is the most common and most damaging feasibility error we see.

## Operating cost model

Worked example: 60 t/d plant, 330 days, 76% extraction, wheat at USD 280/t.

| Item | Basis | Annual cost (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| Wheat | 19,800 t × 280 | 5,544,000 |
| Labour | 12 staff × 330d avg | 95,000 |
| Power | 15,048 t flour × 72 kWh/t, 0.10/kWh | 108,300 |
| Maintenance & spares | 4% of equipment value | 12,000 |
| Packaging | 15,048 t / 0.025 t per bag × 0.35 | 210,672 |
| Overheads | Insurance, admin, transport | 85,000 |
| **Total** | | **6,054,972** |

Revenue at 76% extraction and USD 400/t flour, plus bran revenue:

| Stream | Tonnes | Price | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flour | 15,048 | 400 | 6,019,200 |
| Bran | 3,960 | 120 | 475,200 |
| **Total** | | | **6,494,400** |

Gross margin: USD 439,428 — about 6.8% of revenue.

That is the reality of commodity flour milling. A 6.8% margin on USD 6.5 million of turnover is USD 439,000 a year against a project cost of roughly USD 470,000 for a floor-mounted plant of this size. Payback about a year, which is why every point of extraction and every cent of wheat price matters.

But notice how fragile it is. A 5% rise in wheat price without a matching flour price rise wipes out the entire margin. This is why flour mills hedge, blend wheat origins, and fight for every point of extraction.

### What actually moves the margin

| Lever | Effect on annual profit (60 t/d) |
|---|---|
| +1% extraction | +USD 79,200 |
| −5% wheat cost | +USD 277,000 |
| +3% flour price | +USD 180,576 |
| −10% power consumption | +USD 10,800 |
| −10% labour cost | +USD 9,500 |
| +2% bran recovery | +USD 9,500 |

Extraction is the largest lever you control. Wheat price is the largest lever you do not.

## Break-even and utilisation

Fixed costs dominate the downside. A mill running at 40% utilisation still pays the same labour, insurance, and capital service.

At 60 t/d capacity, fixed cost is roughly USD 190,000 a year (labour, overheads, maintenance, capital service). Variable margin per tonne of flour is approximately USD 25 (flour plus bran revenue minus wheat, power and packaging).

Break-even volume = 190,000 ÷ 25 = **7,600 tonnes of flour per year** = 23 t/day = **38% utilisation**.

That sounds reassuring — you break even at 38% of capacity. But at 38% utilisation you are covering costs and servicing nothing. To service debt and generate a return you need 75–85%.

**The practical rule: do not build a plant you expect to run below 70% utilisation in year one.** If your market analysis says you will sell 60% of a 60 t/d plant's output, build a 40 t/d plant instead.

## Payback reality check

| Scenario | Payback |
|---|---|
| New market, new brand, 60% utilisation year 1 | 6–9 years |
| Existing distribution, 80% utilisation year 1 | 3–4 years |
| Contract milling for a known buyer | 2.5–3.5 years |
| Premium product at 20% price premium | 2–3 years, but volume risk is high |

Anything advertised at 18 months assumes a premium that will not survive competition, or ignores working capital, or ignores the building.

## The five lines buyers forget

**1. Working capital.** 30–45 days of wheat inventory, 30–60 days of receivables, spares. Typically 12–18% of project cost.

**2. Electrical infrastructure.** A 200 t/d plant draws 690 kW. The transformer, switchgear and cabling can cost USD 80,000–150,000 and are almost never in the equipment quotation.

**3. Effluent and dust control.** Required by law nearly everywhere and often omitted from budgets until the permit application.

**4. Spare parts stocking.** 2–3% of equipment value, purchased at commissioning. Without it, a failed bearing becomes a three-week shutdown.

**5. Training and ramp-up.** Most plants run at 60–75% of design capacity for the first three to six months while operators learn. Plan your cash flow for it.

## Financing structures

| Structure | Typical terms | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | — | Small plants, family businesses |
| Bank term loan | 50–70% of project, 5–8 years | Most projects |
| Equipment leasing | 70–90% of equipment, 3–5 years | Cash-flow-sensitive buyers |
| Export credit agency | 60–85%, 7–12 years | Large imports from ECA countries |
| Supplier credit | 20–40% of equipment, 12–24 months | Bridging the installation period |

Supplier credit is negotiable on larger orders and is often the difference between a project proceeding this year and next. Ask.

## What to do before signing

1. **Commission an independent market study** on flour demand within your distribution radius. Not a national statistic — a local one.
2. **Secure wheat supply** or a firm supply agreement before building. A mill without reliable wheat is a warehouse.
3. **Get three quotations on the same flow sheet**, not three quotations of whatever each supplier wanted to sell.
4. **Verify the flow sheet arithmetic** — passages, sifter area, power budget, building height.
5. **Model at 60% utilisation**, not 100%. If the project survives that, it is robust.
6. **Budget the second 55%** — building, electrical, installation, working capital.

## The honest summary

Flour milling is a good business at scale, in markets with real demand, with a flow sheet that extracts properly. It is a bad business for anyone who buys on price, underbudgets the building, and assumes 100% utilisation from day one.

The plant that pays back reliably is the one that costs 20% more than the cheapest quotation and extracts three points more than the cheapest flow sheet.
