A laboratory covering moisture, ash and granulation costs USD 4,000–7,000 and will save several times that in rejected loads alone. Above 100 t/d an NIR analyser pays for itself in technician time. But one properly trained technician is worth more than an extra instrument at any scale.

## Tier 1 — Minimum viable (USD 4,000–7,000)

| Equipment | Approx. cost | Tests enabled |
|---|---|---|
| Moisture oven (105 °C) | USD 400–900 | Moisture |
| Muffle furnace (600 °C) | USD 1,200–2,500 | Ash |
| Analytical balance (0.1 mg) | USD 800–1,800 | All gravimetric |
| Sieve shaker + sieve stack | USD 900–1,800 | Granulation |
| Desiccator, crucibles, glassware | USD 400–800 | Support |

**Enables**: moisture, ash, granulation. These three cover 80% of daily production control.

This tier is sufficient for any mill up to [60 t/d](/flour-milling-plant/40-60tpd-flour-plant/) that is selling to a market without formal certification requirements.

## Tier 2 — Standard (USD 12,000–20,000)

Adds:

| Equipment | Approx. cost | Tests enabled |
|---|---|---|
| Kjeldahl digestion + distillation | USD 6,000–10,000 | Protein |
| Falling number apparatus | USD 2,500–4,000 | Sprout damage / alpha-amylase |
| Water bath, titration kit | USD 500–1,000 | Wet gluten |
| Sample mill (laboratory grinder) | USD 600–1,200 | Sample prep |

**Enables**: protein, falling number, wet gluten.

Needed if you buy wheat on specification, blend origins, or sell to industrial bakeries that audit your QC.

## Tier 3 — Full (USD 45,000–80,000)

Adds:

| Equipment | Approx. cost | Tests enabled |
|---|---|---|
| Farinograph | USD 25,000–40,000 | Water absorption, development, stability |
| Extensograph | USD 18,000–30,000 | Resistance, extensibility |
| Gluten washer | USD 3,000–6,000 | Wet/dry gluten |
| Colour grader | USD 2,000–4,000 | Flour colour (L*a*b*) |

**Enables**: full rheological specification and product development.

Justified above 200 t/d, or by any mill selling to a specification that names farinograph values.

## The NIR alternative

A near-infrared analyser measures moisture, protein and ash in about 30 seconds, with no consumables and minimal sample prep.

| Consideration | NIR | Wet chemistry |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | USD 18,000–45,000 | Included in Tier 1/2 |
| Time per test | 30 seconds | 1.5–5 hours |
| Consumables | ~0 | Reagents, crucibles, gas |
| Accuracy | ±0.05% ash, ±0.15% protein | ±0.01% |
| Calibration | Required periodically, per wheat class | None |

NIR pays back above 100 t/d because it lets you test every hour instead of every shift, and it releases technician time from bench work to process investigation.

Keep a muffle furnace as reference regardless — NIR calibration drifts and you need wet-chemistry verification.

## Laboratory layout

| Area | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bench work | 3–4 m of bench | Balance on anti-vibration table |
| Furnace area | 1.5 × 1.5 m | Ventilated, fire-rated wall |
| Sample storage | 2 m shelving | Retain 500 g per lot for 30 days |
| Record keeping | Desk | Paper or LIMS |

Total 15–25 m². Locate away from the mill floor — dust ingress destroys balances and clogs furnaces.

## Staff

| Mill capacity | Lab staff |
|---|---|
| Up to 60 t/d | 1 technician per shift (may share duties) |
| 60–200 t/d | 1 per shift + 1 supervisor |
| 200 t/d+ | 2 per shift + supervisor + assistant |

Train the technician in sampling method as carefully as in instrument operation. **Most quality failures are sampling failures.** A perfect test on an unrepresentative sample is worse than no test, because it is trusted.

## Record keeping

Minimum records per production lot:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Lot number | Traceability |
| Date and shift | Attribution |
| Wheat origin and intake lot | Root cause |
| Moisture, ash, protein | Specification |
| Granulation (if tested) | Behaviour prediction |
| Retained sample location | Complaint investigation |
| Operator | Accountability |

Retain samples 30 days minimum, 90 days for export markets.

## Certification readiness

If you intend to sell to large retail or export, your lab needs:

- Documented sampling procedures
- Calibrated instruments with records
- Duplicate testing protocol
- Control samples and trend charts
- Traceability from wheat lot to finished lot

ISO 22000 or FSSC 22000 audits examine the laboratory first, because it is where claims are verified.
