Most performance guarantees in milling contracts are unenforceable, and the reason is almost never bad faith. It is that the guarantee was written as a promise rather than as a test.

"Capacity of 60 tonnes per 24 hours" is a promise. "60 tonnes of flour per 24 hours at 76% extraction on wheat of 12.5% moisture and 12.0% protein, measured over 72 continuous hours with mass balance by weighbridge and packed output" is a test.

Only the second can be failed, and only a guarantee that can be failed is worth anything.

## The three figures that can be measured

Everything else in a milling contract is a specification. These three are performance.

**Capacity.** Tonnes of flour per 24 hours, at a stated extraction, on a stated wheat. All three conditions are required. Capacity quoted at 78% extraction on hard wheat is a different number from capacity at 72% on soft wheat, and the difference is not small.

**Specific power.** kWh per tonne of flour, measured at the main incomer over the test period, divided by flour produced — not wheat milled. This is where contracts quietly diverge: a guarantee written per tonne of wheat looks 25% better and means something else entirely.

**Ash at extraction.** Ash percentage in the finished flour at the guaranteed extraction. This is the figure that decides whether the flow sheet was designed honestly, because anyone can hit a low ash by milling less.

| Figure | Test method | Common trap |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | Weighbridge wheat in, packed flour out, 72 h | Quoted at a lower extraction than the guarantee assumes |
| Specific power | kWh at incomer ÷ flour tonnes | Denominator is wheat, not flour |
| Ash at extraction | Composite sample, 550–600 °C | Measured at a different extraction than guaranteed |

## The wheat clause is the guarantee

This is the point buyers miss most often, and it decides everything.

A mill designed for 12% protein wheat will not hit its capacity on 10.5% wheat, and no supplier can make it. Soft wheat grinds differently, sifts differently, and yields a different extraction at the same ash.

So the guarantee must fix the raw material. The clause needs moisture, protein, test weight, hardness class and impurity level — the same five parameters a wheat contract uses. If the clause says "on commercially clean wheat," nothing is guaranteed, because every wheat is commercially clean to the seller.

Practically: agree the wheat specification, then buy the acceptance-test wheat to that specification, and store it separately so it cannot be blended. Both parties should witness the sampling.

## Run it continuously, sample it properly

A one-hour demonstration at full rate proves the plant can run. It does not prove it can sustain.

The standard for a plant above [60 t/d](/flour-milling-plant/40-60tpd-flour-plant/) is 72 hours continuous. Below 30 t/d, 24 hours is normal. During the run:

- Wheat in by weighbridge ticket, every load
- Flour out by packed weight, every bag or every bulk ticket
- Power by kWh meter reading at start, at 24-hour intervals, and at end
- Flour samples hourly, composited per shift, analysed for ash, moisture and protein
- Downtime logged with cause, and excluded from the calculation only if it is a buyer-side utility failure

That last point matters. Supplier-side stoppages during acceptance count against the guarantee. Buyer-side power cuts do not, but they must be documented at the time, not reconstructed afterwards.

## Retention is the only leverage

Once the plant is handed over and the balance is paid, a failed guarantee becomes a conversation. Suppliers are not obliged to be unreasonable, but the buyer has no mechanism.

The mechanism is retention: 5–10% held for 30–90 days after acceptance, released on the test result. It is standard in industrial contracting and it is routinely dropped in milling contracts to close a price negotiation.

If a supplier refuses retention entirely, that tells you something about their confidence in the test — or about their cash position. Either is worth knowing before you sign.

## What I would insist on

Name the wheat. Define the test duration. Put the power denominator in writing as tonnes of flour. Hold retention.

Four clauses, a page of contract, and they are the difference between a guarantee and a sentence.
