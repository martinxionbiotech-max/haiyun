## The premium

| Channel | Premium over roller flour |
|---|---|
| Health food retail | 1.8–2.2× |
| Artisan bakery supply | 1.5–1.8× |
| Organic certified | 2.0–2.6× |
| Export (EU, North America) | 1.8–2.5× |
| Supermarket private label | 1.3–1.5× |
| Commodity wholesale | 1.0–1.1× (no premium) |

The premium is real and durable, but it is **channel-dependent**. Selling stone flour into a commodity wholesale channel earns you nothing but a higher cost base.

## Why the premium exists

Consumers buy four things:

1. **A process story** — cold stone grinding, slow, traditional
2. **A nutritional claim** — retained vitamins, whole grain
3. **A sensory difference** — coarser texture, stronger flavour, visible bran
4. **A values signal** — small mill, additive-free, local

None of these are about price. All of them require the package, the label and the shelf position to communicate them. Flour in an unprinted 25 kg sack has no premium regardless of how it was milled.

## Volume reality

| Channel | Monthly volume | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Single artisan bakery | 0.3–1 t | Uses it in own products |
| Bakery chain (10 outlets) | 2–5 t | Contract supply |
| Regional health-food distributor | 2–5 t | Reaches 20–50 shops |
| National health-food brand | 20–80 t | Requires certification and audits |
| Export container | 18–24 t | One-off, high working capital |

Note the gap between 5 t/month and 20 t/month. Most mills that fail did so by installing capacity for the second number while having access only to the first.

## Matching capacity to the market

| Configuration | Monthly output | Market needed |
|---|---|---|
| SFM-100 | 8–10 t | One bakery plus direct sales |
| SFM-1+2 | 35–40 t | Distributor plus bakery chain plus retail |
| SFM-3+4 | 100–110 t | National brand or export contracts |

If you cannot name the buyers for 60% of that volume before installation, install one size smaller.

## Building the story

What actually moves stone flour:

| Element | Why it works |
|---|---|
| "Stone ground at 28 rpm" | Specific, verifiable, visual |
| Mill photographs | Authenticity signal |
| Batch date and mill number | Traceability sells |
| Whole grain claim with extraction stated | Transparency |
| Nutritional panel showing fibre | Functional benefit |
| Recipe on pack | Reduces barrier to trial |

What does not work: "premium quality", "traditional methods", "healthiest flour". These are claims every competitor also makes.

## Certification

| Certification | Cost | Premium uplift |
|---|---|---|
| Organic | USD 2,000–8,000/yr | +20–40% |
| Non-GMO | USD 1,000–3,000/yr | +5–10% |
| Whole Grain Stamp | USD 500–2,000/yr | +5–8% |
| ISO 22000 / FSSC | USD 6,000–15,000/yr | Required for large retail |

Certification pays when you are selling into a channel that requires it. It does not create a premium on its own.

## The distribution-first rule

The correct sequence:

1. Identify 3–5 target buyers by name
2. Get sample acceptance and a price indication
3. Estimate monthly volume across those buyers
4. Install capacity at 60–70% of that estimate
5. Add capacity when you are sold out for three consecutive months

The wrong sequence, which is common: install SFM-3+4, produce 100 t/month, discover the market is 15 t/month, and watch inventory go rancid.
